Using the editor

Models, textures and sound files

Put files anywhere in the project folder and they show up in the Assets panel. These are the formats Empire Engine reads and what it reads from each.

Prefer glTF

glTF is the most complete path: full PBR materials, transparency, a node hierarchy, skins and animations. Blender exports it with File > Export > glTF 2.0.

glTF (.glb, .gltf)

Both binary .glb and text .gltf load. A .gltf file's buffer can be a data: URI or a file next to it.

Materials

  • Base color factor and texture
  • Metallic and roughness factors and the metallic-roughness texture
  • Normal map (with its scale) and occlusion map (with its strength)
  • Emissive factor and texture, with KHR_materials_emissive_strength
  • KHR_materials_ior and KHR_materials_specular
  • Vertex colors, which multiply the base color

Textures can be embedded in the .glb, given as data: URIs, or sit in files next to the model. Each image is decoded once, however many materials use it.

Transparency

The glTF alphaMode decides how a material handles alpha:

  • OPAQUE (the default): alpha is ignored.
  • MASK: pixels with less alpha than alphaCutoff are not drawn. Good for leaves, fences and grass.
  • BLEND: see-through. The surface is blended over what's behind it, after all solid surfaces are drawn, and doesn't hide what's behind it.

Animation and skins

Models with animations or skins keep their node hierarchy and joint weights, and their animation clips can be played with an Animator. In the Assets panel such a model has a small arrow; click it to list its animations.

Not supported yet

Sparse accessors, texture transforms (KHR_texture_transform) and morph targets.

FBX

Binary FBX version 7.x, which Blender, Maya, 3ds Max and most asset stores produce. Meshes are placed with their node transforms and converted to Y-up meters using the file's own axis and unit settings, so a model looks the same as its glTF export. Each material on a mesh becomes its own part.

  • Read: diffuse color, opacity, emission, roughness (from shininess), and the diffuse, normal and emissive texture maps, embedded or next to the file.
  • Not read: ASCII FBX, skinning, blend shapes, animation. Use glTF for animated characters.

OBJ

Wavefront .obj with the .mtl libraries it names. Coordinates are used as they are: Y up, one unit per meter (Blender's defaults). Each object/group and material pair becomes one part.

  • Read from .mtl: Kd (base color), d/Tr (opacity), Ke (emission), Pr/Pm (roughness/metallic), Ns (roughness, when Pr is missing), and the maps map_Kd, map_Bump/bump/norm and map_Ke, found next to the .mtl or in a textures folder there.
  • Not read: vertex colors, curves, line continuations.

Sound files

MP3 and WAV are decoded once into 16-bit samples in memory, so playing a sound never touches the disk. Sounds with more than two channels keep only the first two. See Audio.

Using files in the editor

  • Models: drag onto the scene to place one, or pick one with a Mesh component's model button.
  • Sounds: drag onto an Audio Source's Clip field, or onto the scene to make an object that plays it.
  • Animations: open a model's arrow in the Assets panel and drag an animation onto an Animator entry's Clip field.
  • Sky images: pick one for an Environment's panorama.

Models reload automatically within a second of their file changing, so you can keep a modelling tool open and re-export.
